The Anatomy of Album Sales

Modeling the diffusion of Everclear’s Sparkle and Fade

Alexandre Hopman-Ruh

Released in May 1995, Everclear’s Sparkle and Fade followed a three-phase commercial path: an initial plateau, a mid-cycle trough, and a Christmas breakthrough that permanently shifted the weekly sales level. Radio airplay explains the broad trend but not the discontinuity at week 31.

Working within a timing-of-first-purchase framework, the paper builds from baseline Pareto II and Burr XII hazard models through radio-airplay covariates to a two-segment mixture. The preferred Burr XII two-segment model separates a small cohort of genre-committed fans, who buy on recognition when the lead single reaches their radio, from a much larger mainstream cohort, who convert only once cumulative exposure and chart visibility build. It gives the strongest fit and validation evidence of the models considered, and implies roughly 125,000 units across the four withheld weeks of March 1996.
